# Question

If two traditions both claim that God revealed truth, quoting one tradition's scripture against the other cannot by itself settle the dispute. A comparison needs standards that are not simply "my revelation says my revelation is true."

Possible criteria to examine include:

- historical proximity to the events being described;
- multiple or independent lines of testimony;
- textual transmission and whether the relevant text can be reconstructed;
- continuity with earlier revelation claims;
- explanatory power and coherence;
- fulfilled prophecy, where the prediction and fulfillment can be independently established;
- miracle claims and the background assumptions used to assess them;
- the character and credibility of prophetic witnesses;
- internal consistency;
- whether a tradition can explain the strongest evidence claimed by its rivals without special pleading.

No single criterion should be assumed decisive in advance. Later nodes should also examine whether a religion is entitled to use internal evidence after some independent warrant for the tradition has already been established.
