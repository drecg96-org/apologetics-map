# Question

The objection is stronger when it is framed carefully:

> If God has maximal power and is the one who sets the terms of reconciliation, why should forgiveness require the death of Christ at all?

Calling the cross "necessary" can mean different things.

It could mean an **external necessity**: some law or power above God forced God to demand blood before he was allowed to forgive.

Or it could mean a **chosen or fitting necessity within God's redemptive purpose**: given what God intends to accomplish through incarnation, judgment, self-giving love, victory over evil, moral transformation, or reconciliation, the cross is the means by which God chooses to accomplish it.

Those are not the same claim.

## What the next move must address

A serious answer cannot merely say "sin has a price." It needs to explain why an omnipotent God would not simply cancel that price, and it should acknowledge that Christian theories of atonement disagree about exactly what the cross accomplishes and why it is necessary.
