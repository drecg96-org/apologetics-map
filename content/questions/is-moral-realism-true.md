# Main question

Before asking what grounds morality, first ask whether there is anything objective to ground.

The central divide is:

- **Realist answer:** at least some moral claims, values, reasons, or obligations are valid independently of what individuals or societies happen to approve.
- **Anti-realist answer:** morality is ultimately attitude-dependent, constructed, non-cognitive, systematically in error, or otherwise not a domain of stance-independent moral facts.

## Why this is the root of the morality map

Arguments about God, naturalism, necessary normative facts, or divine commands belong downstream. They compare explanations of moral reality only after some form of moral realism is in play.

The first-stage debate therefore asks whether realism survives challenges from subjectivism, disagreement, queerness, evolutionary debunking, non-cognitivism, and error theory, while also considering positive arguments from moral experience, fallibility, progress, categorical reasons, and epistemic normativity.
