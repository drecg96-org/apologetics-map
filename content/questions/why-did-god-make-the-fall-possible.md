# Question

The Fall creates a deeper version of the free-will problem.

God is not surprised by the possibility of rebellion. On classical theism, God knows what creatures can do and has the power not to create them at all.

So why create a world containing a genuine route to catastrophic moral failure?

A familiar Christian answer appeals to goods such as meaningful agency, love, moral responsibility, and genuine relationship. But that answer has to do more than say "free will is good." It has to explain why those goods are worth the risk and why God could not secure them in a world where free creatures always choose rightly.
