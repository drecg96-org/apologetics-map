# Question

Islam presents itself as standing in continuity with earlier prophets while correcting beliefs it regards as later distortions. The sharpest comparison with Christianity appears where the Qur'an recharacterizes Jesus, rejects his divinity, and gives an account of the crucifixion that differs from the Christian tradition.

A useful evaluation should ask:

- What exactly does the Qur'an claim was revealed previously?
- What does it claim went wrong in Jewish or Christian transmission or interpretation?
- Is textual corruption, interpretive corruption, or both being alleged?
- What evidence exists for the content of Jewish and Christian belief before Islam?
- When a later revelation conflicts with earlier historical sources, what independent reason should privilege the later revelation?
- Conversely, could a genuine later revelation correct an earlier community even if surviving earlier sources disagree with it?
- What evidence supports Muhammad's prophetic authority independently of assuming the Qur'an is divine?

This branch should eventually fan out into manuscript history, Qur'anic formation, hadith and sira, prophecy claims, textual transmission, and specific historical conflicts.
