# Crux question

Suppose a society broadly approves a practice that another society condemns. If social approval itself constitutes moral truth, can the first society be morally mistaken rather than merely different?

This question separates two claims that are easy to blur:

- **Social facts influence moral beliefs and law.** This is compatible with objective morality.
- **Social approval makes moral claims true.** This is a metaethical claim about what morality is.

## If the answer is yes

If an entire society can be morally mistaken about something it approves, social consensus cannot be the complete standard of moral truth. The conversation can then ask what standard permits the society itself to be judged mistaken.

## If the answer is no

The view accepts a society-relative implication: condemnation across societies is ultimately made from a standpoint rather than by appeal to a stance-independent moral fact. That is a coherent anti-realist option if its commitments are accepted.

## Limit

This question does not prove moral realism. Its purpose is to expose the choice clearly enough that both sides know which position is actually being defended.
