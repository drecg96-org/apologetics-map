# Question

A later revelation is not disqualified merely because it is later. If God reveals something later, it could correct an earlier community.

But when a later revelation makes a claim about an earlier public event that conflicts with sources much closer to that event, the comparison needs more than chronology in either direction.

Useful tests include:

- independent reasons to trust the later messenger or revelation;
- the quality and proximity of the earlier historical evidence;
- whether the later text claims corruption of the earlier text, corruption of interpretation, or a new overriding disclosure;
- whether the correction explains the earlier evidence better than rival accounts;
- whether the same standard is applied consistently when the later revelation confirms rather than corrects earlier scripture.

This question is deliberately tradition-neutral.
