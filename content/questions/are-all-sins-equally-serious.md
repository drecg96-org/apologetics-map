# Question

A common Christian slogan says, "all sins are equal in God's eyes." Taken literally, that is too strong.

Christianity more commonly holds two claims together:

1. **All people are sinners and no one earns reconciliation with God by being morally flawless.**
2. **Not every sin is equally grave, equally culpable, equally harmful, or judged in exactly the same way.**

Confusing those claims creates unnecessary problems in conversations about punishment.

## Why people say "all sins are equal"

The slogan often tries to express a truth about universal guilt and the universal need for grace: murderers, liars, adulterers, the proud, and everyone else stand in need of forgiveness.

But "everyone needs grace" does not entail "every wrongful act has exactly the same moral weight."

## Next question

Once that distinction is made, the useful question becomes: **how should divine judgment, moral gravity, and civil punishment relate to one another?**
