# Question

Judaism, Christianity, and Islam all affirm one God, but Christianity also worships Jesus and confesses Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Judaism and Islam therefore raise a fundamental challenge: **does Christianity preserve monotheism or redefine it beyond recognition?**

This branch should distinguish several questions that are often collapsed:

- Did Jesus make or accept claims that imply divine status?
- Did the earliest Christians worship or invoke Jesus, and how early did that happen?
- Can "one God in three persons" be stated without contradiction or tritheism?
- Does Jewish monotheism permit any categories of divine agency, Word, Wisdom, Spirit, or exalted figures that help explain early Christology?
- Does the Qur'an reject the actual classical doctrine of the Trinity or a different conception of divine plurality?
- Even if the Trinity is logically coherent, what positive reason is there to believe it?

The Christian task is not merely to call the Trinity a mystery; it is to show both that the doctrine is not a contradiction and that there is sufficient reason for it to arise from the relevant evidence.
