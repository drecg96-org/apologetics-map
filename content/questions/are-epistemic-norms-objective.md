# Crux question

Are there objective norms governing belief and reasoning—for example, that one ought to follow the evidence, avoid contradictions, and not knowingly reason fallaciously?

## If the answer is yes

Objective normativity has been admitted somewhere. The next question is why objective facts about what one ought to believe are possible while objective facts about what one ought to do are ruled out in principle.

This does not collapse epistemic and moral norms into the same thing. It asks for a relevant difference between them.

## If the answer is no

The position extends anti-realism beyond morality to epistemic normativity. Claims about what someone rationally ought to believe must then be reconstructed in non-objective terms as well.

## Why this branch matters

This is especially useful against a broad "normative facts are too strange to exist" objection. It is less useful against an anti-realist who already has a developed account of epistemic normativity or who denies objective epistemic norms too.

## Limit

The existence of objective epistemic norms would not prove objective moral norms. The point is comparative: a general rejection of normativity cannot be applied selectively to morality without explaining the asymmetry.
