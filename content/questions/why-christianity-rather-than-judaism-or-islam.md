# Question

Judaism, Christianity, and Islam share important theological ancestry, but they make incompatible claims about Jesus, revelation, covenant, and the identity of God.

The useful question is therefore not simply, "Which religion feels most convincing?" It is:

**Which disputed claims are most capable of distinguishing the traditions, and what kind of evidence should decide among them?**

## Recommended route

Rather than comparing every doctrine at once, begin with the claims that create the largest downstream differences:

- Was Jesus the promised Messiah?
- Was Jesus actually crucified and raised from the dead?
- Is Jesus divine, and can the Trinity be a genuinely monotheistic doctrine?
- Does the Christian movement continue Israel's covenantal story or break from it?
- Does the Qur'an provide a warranted later correction of Christian claims about Jesus?
- How should later revelations be tested against earlier texts and historically accessible events?

These questions can be investigated separately.

## Method

This branch should use four kinds of evidence where available:

1. each tradition's own primary texts;
2. sources from informed representatives of that tradition;
3. historical and textual scholarship that does not depend on accepting the religion's authority;
4. philosophical analysis of coherence, revelation, miracle claims, and religious disagreement.

The goal is comparative evaluation, not merely collecting Christian answers.
