# Question

Suppose at least some moral claims are objectively true. What makes those claims true, and what explains the authority of moral obligations?

This is a **downstream grounding question**. It is different from asking whether moral realism is true in the first place, how people discover moral truths, why people feel motivated to act morally, or whether religious people behave better.

## Gate before entering this branch

The conversation should normally reach this node only after moral realism has at least been provisionally granted.

If the other person instead accepts error theory, non-cognitivism, or another form of anti-realism, return to the upstream realism/anti-realism branches. Those positions deny or reinterpret the thing this question is asking us to ground.

## Main realist routes

Live answers include:

- a necessarily good personal foundation or theistic account;
- robust non-theistic moral realism, where at least some normative facts are necessary or fundamental;
- naturalistic moral realism, which identifies or grounds moral facts in relevant natural facts;
- constructivist or rationalist accounts that attempt to explain normativity through a privileged rational standpoint or procedure.

The moral argument for God is therefore one proposed answer to this question, not the only answer.

## Important distinction

Even if one account best explains moral **value**, it may still owe a separate account of moral **obligation**. The map should therefore ask both what makes things good or bad and what makes some reasons genuinely authoritative for agents.
