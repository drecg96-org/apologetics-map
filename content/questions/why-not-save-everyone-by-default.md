# Question

If nobody earns salvation, the natural follow-up is:

> Why does God not simply save everyone?

The question becomes sharper when Christian texts are also read as saying that God desires people to be saved.

That produces a genuine theological tension between:

- God's saving will,
- God's power,
- the claim that salvation is grace,
- the role of repentance, faith, or freely received communion,
- and the fact that many Christian traditions deny that every person will finally be saved.

## Main branches

Christian answers divide here.

Some emphasize human freedom and say saving communion cannot simply be imposed on a person who finally refuses God.

Some emphasize divine sovereignty and explain the limitation in other terms.

Christian universalists argue that God's grace can eventually succeed with every person without needing to violate freedom.

The map should represent that disagreement rather than treating one answer as the only Christian option.
