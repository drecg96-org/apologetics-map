# Question

Suppose, for the sake of argument, that a person can still repent after death.

Would a direct encounter with God make rejection impossible?

That depends on a distinction between:

- **knowing that God exists**, and
- **wanting communion with God on God's terms**.

A free-will defense can say clearer knowledge removes one source of nonbelief without automatically transforming pride, hatred, self-rule, resentment, or other dispositions of the will.

A universalist can push back that an all-loving and all-knowing God may have unlimited ways and time to heal those dispositions without coercing the person.

So the real disagreement becomes:

> Can a free person remain opposed to the good forever once deception, ignorance, and confusion are progressively removed?
