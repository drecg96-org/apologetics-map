# Question

This is the key religion-discriminating step.

Suppose, for the sake of argument, that rationality is difficult to ground without a necessary, rational, personal source of reality. Why should the conclusion be **Christianity** rather than generic classical theism, Judaism, Islam, another monotheistic system, or a philosophical idealism?

A Van Tilian can answer that TAG was never meant to infer a bare deity first and Christianity later. The whole Christian worldview is presented as the alleged precondition from the beginning.

But that statement describes the **intended conclusion**. It does not by itself show that the conclusion is warranted.

## The discriminating burden

To establish Christianity specifically, the argument must show why relevant rival worldviews fail at the same transcendental task.

That means asking concrete questions: Can Judaism or Islam ground the same rational and moral order? Does the Trinity perform indispensable explanatory work? If Christianity and Islam offer conflicting accounts of revelation and Jesus, what independent considerations adjudicate between them? Does historical evidence—especially the crucifixion and resurrection—remain necessary?

Those questions make the Christianity-specific step testable.
