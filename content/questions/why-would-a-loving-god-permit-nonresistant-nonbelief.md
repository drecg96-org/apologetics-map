# Main question

The strongest version of divine hiddenness is not:

> "Why doesn't God make himself spectacularly obvious to everyone?"

It is narrower:

> **If a perfectly loving God wants relationship with every willing person, why do some people who appear sincerely open to God remain unable to believe that God exists?**

Philosophers often call this **nonresistant nonbelief**.

## Why this is a real challenge

Schellenberg's argument can be simplified:

1. A perfectly loving God would be open to meaningful relationship with every capable person.
2. A person normally cannot enter a conscious reciprocal relationship with someone they do not believe exists.
3. Some capable people appear not to resist God and yet remain nonbelievers.
4. Therefore, their nonbelief is evidence against the existence of a perfectly loving God.

The Christian response therefore needs more than "some people suppress the truth." Even if resistant unbelief exists, the argument concerns the hardest case: the person who would welcome God if they could honestly become convinced.

## Better than asking why God *must* be hidden

Christianity does not need to prove that God is **required** to remain hidden in every respect.

It needs to answer the more defensible question:

> **Could a perfectly loving God have morally sufficient reasons to permit some degree of epistemic distance, including at least some cases of sincere nonbelief?**

That opens several distinct lines rather than forcing everything into one free-will answer:

- perhaps overwhelming awareness of divine judgment would distort morally significant choice;
- perhaps God seeks morally transforming relationship rather than bare assent to a proposition;
- perhaps relationship with God can begin through seeking, trust, lament, or response before full propositional belief;
- perhaps perfect love does not entail making conscious reciprocal relationship immediately available at every moment.

Each response has limits. A serious map should test them rather than treating "God values free will" as a complete answer.
