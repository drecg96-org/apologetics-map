# Salvation and Hell

This topic starts with a common fairness objection:

> Suppose someone lives an unusually good life, sincerely does not believe God exists, and would be open to God if convinced. Why should that person be excluded from heaven?

The strongest version of the objection should not assume the person secretly hates God or knowingly rejects something they believe to be true.

The line then separates several issues that are often compressed into the phrase **"God sends unbelievers to hell"**:

1. Christianity does not normally describe heaven as a reward earned by enough moral performance.
2. Some accounts of hell emphasize **divine judgment and punishment**.
3. Other accounts emphasize **freely chosen separation or self-exclusion from God**.
4. Some accounts combine the two: a person's settled orientation is genuinely chosen, while God's final judgment genuinely confirms and judges it.
5. The self-exclusion model faces a serious follow-up: **what about sincere or nonresistant nonbelief?**
6. Christian traditions do not all answer that final question in the same way.
