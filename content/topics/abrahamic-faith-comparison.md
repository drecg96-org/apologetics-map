# Christianity, Judaism, and Islam

This topic asks a narrower question than "Does God exist?": **if some form of Abrahamic monotheism is true, what would distinguish Christianity from Judaism and Islam?**

The comparison should not begin by assuming Christian theology. It should first identify the claims on which the traditions actually diverge, represent each tradition from sources that speak in its own voice, and then ask what historical, textual, philosophical, and theological evidence bears on those claims.

## Main discriminator questions

1. **Jesus as Messiah:** Does Jesus fit the Jewish expectations relevant to the Messiah, and how should Christians answer the objection that the promised messianic age did not arrive in the expected way?
2. **Resurrection:** Was Jesus crucified and raised from the dead? Christianity makes this central, while Islam offers a materially different account of the crucifixion and Judaism does not accept the Christian resurrection claim.
3. **Divine identity and monotheism:** Is devotion to Jesus compatible with the worship of one God? This opens the Trinity, incarnation, and early-Christian-devotion branches.
4. **Covenant and Torah:** Does Christianity fulfill Israel's covenantal story, depart from it, or improperly supersede it?
5. **Later revelation:** Does Islam provide a warranted correction and continuation of earlier revelation, or does it conflict with evidence closer to the events it reinterprets?
6. **How revelations are compared:** What standards should be used when multiple traditions claim continuity with Abraham, prophecy, scripture, and divine revelation?

## Editorial rule for this branch

Separate three tasks that are often blurred together:

- **description:** what Judaism, Christianity, or Islam actually teaches;
- **internal coherence:** whether that teaching fits the rest of the tradition's own commitments;
- **truth assessment:** what independent historical, philosophical, textual, or experiential reasons support one account over another.

A Jewish or Muslim source is not included merely as an objection to Christianity; it should be used to state that tradition's own case accurately. Christian responses should then answer the strongest version of the claim rather than a simplified substitute.

The discriminator branches now fan out into arguments, objections, responses, shared historical evidence, and primary or representative sources. Shared questions—especially the resurrection evidence—reuse the existing graph rather than creating religion-specific duplicates.
