# Claim

Rational thought is not exhausted by one mental event causing another.

To count as **reasoning**, there must be some distinction between inferences that are warranted and inferences that are not. To count as **knowledge**, thought must also connect with a reality sufficiently ordered and accessible for beliefs to be true and responsibly formed.

This suggests at least three kinds of conditions:

- normative standards of correct inference;
- meaningful concepts or predication;
- and a sufficiently dependable fit between cognitive agents and the world they attempt to know.

## Limit

This claim does not establish that those conditions require God. Platonist, naturalist, pragmatist, reliabilist, and other accounts may attempt to explain some or all of them.
