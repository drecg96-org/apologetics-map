# Claim

The ambitious conclusion of a Van Tilian transcendental argument is not merely:

> A rational deity would help explain rationality.

It is that **Christian theism is actually necessary for intelligible thought**.

That conclusion is stronger than ordinary inference to the best explanation. If it succeeds, rejecting Christian theism while continuing to reason would depend on conditions that the rejection cannot itself make sense of.

## Burden of proof

Because the conclusion is so strong, the argument must establish both sides of the comparison:

1. Christianity really does provide the required account.
2. relevant rival worldviews really do fail in a way that cannot be repaired.

It must also answer the Stroudian concern that a transcendental argument may establish only what thinkers must presuppose rather than what exists independently of their thought.
