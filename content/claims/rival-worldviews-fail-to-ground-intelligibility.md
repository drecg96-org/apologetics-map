# Claim

The strongest form of TAG requires more than showing that Christianity can tell a coherent story about reason.

It additionally claims that relevant rival worldviews **cannot** supply an adequate account of the same conditions without inconsistency, arbitrariness, or borrowing resources unavailable on their own terms.

That is a much stronger claim than saying Christianity has explanatory advantages. It needs comparative arguments against actual rival positions.

## What a real comparison has to explain

A serious comparison should ask how each worldview handles at least five features:

1. **Logical and modal truth:** why logical consequence and necessity are objective rather than merely descriptions of local human habits.
2. **Epistemic normativity:** why some beliefs and inferences are rationally warranted while others are not.
3. **Cognitive reliability:** why the faculties used to form a worldview are sufficiently truth-conducive to deserve reflective trust.
4. **Mind-world fit:** why finite minds can successfully discover stable, intelligible structure in reality.
5. **Religion-specific grounding:** if a rival also has a rational Creator, revelation, and providence, what specifically makes the Christian account uniquely adequate?

## Comparative pressure points

### Naturalism: normativity

Naturalism can describe causal processes that produce beliefs. TAG presses a further question: what grounds the **normative** difference between a belief produced by a cause and a belief one *ought* to accept because the evidence supports it?

Cuneo's work illustrates how difficult it is to eliminate epistemic normativity without threatening ordinary rational evaluation. Contemporary naturalists respond with several realist, reductionist, functional, and reasons-based accounts, so this is a genuine dispute rather than a settled refutation.

### Naturalism: cognitive reliability

Plantinga-style arguments press whether unguided evolution gives a reflective naturalist adequate reason to trust cognition aimed at truth rather than merely adaptive behavior.

But evolutionary epistemology supplies an important reply: tracking real environmental structure can itself have fitness value. The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore that naturalism faces questions about the **scope and depth** of its reliability account, especially for abstract reasoning—not that evolution plainly makes reliable cognition impossible.

### Platonism and abstract truth

Platonism can preserve objective, necessary logical or mathematical truth without appealing to God. That makes it a serious rival, not an obvious failure.

The classic Benacerraf-style pressure point is epistemological: if abstract objects are causally isolated, what explains reliable human access to them? This creates a real mind-to-abstracta problem, but contemporary Platonists have responses. The challenge is not equivalent to a demonstration that Platonism is incoherent.

### Logic as primitive, structural, or plural

Anderson and Welty argue that necessary logical truths are best understood as contents of a necessary divine mind.

Critics such as Malpass deny that the move from necessary truths to necessarily existing **thoughts** has been established. In addition, logical pluralism complicates appeals to "the laws of logic" as though the relevant object of explanation were philosophically undisputed.

A Christian TAG therefore needs to show not merely that divine conceptualism is available, but why primitive, structural, abstract, or pluralist alternatives ultimately fail.

### Rival monotheisms

Judaism and Islam can also appeal to a transcendent rational Creator, providence, revelation, and an ordered creation.

That creates a symmetry problem for a **Christian-specific** TAG. The defender must identify concrete doctrines within the rival system that undermine the proposed conditions of intelligibility and explain why the Christian system avoids the same problem. Merely repeating that Christianity is self-attesting does not perform that comparative work.

## What the evidence currently warrants

The evidence supports a **modest comparative conclusion**:

> Major rival accounts face serious and still-disputed questions about normativity, abstract truth, cognitive reliability, epistemic access, or the unity of these features.

It does **not** currently justify treating the universal claim—"every relevant non-Christian worldview fails, and Christianity alone succeeds"—as a result established by philosophical consensus.

For the ambitious Van Tilian conclusion to succeed, the remaining alternatives must be shown to be not merely less satisfying or less unified, but actually unable to supply the required conditions on their own terms.

## Strong objections that remain live

The principal counterarguments are substantial:

- naturalistic evolutionary epistemology offers mechanisms for substantial truth-tracking;
- secular realists can take objective normative reasons as fundamental rather than divine;
- Platonists can treat necessary abstract truths as mind-independent and answer the epistemic-access objection in several ways;
- logical truths need not obviously be divine thoughts, and logical pluralism complicates the explanandum;
- Stroud's challenge asks whether transcendental necessity establishes mind-independent reality or only a required conceptual commitment;
- Jewish and Muslim worldviews can make parallel Creator-and-revelation grounding claims.

These objections do not automatically refute TAG. They establish the burden that a strong Christian-specific TAG still has to meet.
