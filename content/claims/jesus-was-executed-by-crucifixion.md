# Claim

Jesus of Nazareth was executed by Roman crucifixion and died.

This is an ordinary historical claim rather than the resurrection conclusion. A resurrection argument needs genuine death as part of the explanandum; a survival or swoon hypothesis disputes this premise or its implications rather than explaining a resurrection after death.

## Interreligious relevance

Mainstream historical judgment and the later Qur'anic account create a direct comparative-religion question because Qur'an 4:157 is commonly read as denying that Jesus was killed by crucifixion.

The historical claim and the theological interpretation should therefore be assessed separately.
