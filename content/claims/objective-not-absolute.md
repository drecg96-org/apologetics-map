# Claim

Three different questions should not be collapsed into one:

- **Objective:** Is the truth or authority of the moral judgment independent of an individual's or society's approval?
- **Absolute:** Does the same rule apply regardless of circumstances?
- **Universal:** Does the rule apply to every person, or is it universally believed?

A moral realist can therefore say that a judgment is objectively correct in a particular situation while also holding that different circumstances could change what ought to be done.

## Why this matters

Many quick objections to "objective morality" actually target exceptionless rules or universal agreement. Those objections miss the narrower metaethical claim at issue in this branch.

This clarification does not establish moral realism. It only defines the claim more carefully.
