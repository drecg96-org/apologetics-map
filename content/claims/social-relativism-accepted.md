# Accepted commitment

A person can consistently reject stance-independent moral truth and accept the consequences of doing so. At that point, repeating increasingly uncomfortable examples does not by itself refute the position.

## What this commits the view to

On the society-relative version of the position:

- A society cannot be shown morally mistaken merely by pointing out that its approved norms differ from ours.
- Cross-society condemnation can remain forceful, but it is condemnation from a moral standpoint rather than discovery of a society-independent moral error.
- "Moral progress" can be described as movement toward values we endorse, greater coherence, reduced suffering, broader inclusion, or another chosen standard, rather than necessarily as discovering an independently true moral order.
- Law can reflect and enforce a society's prevailing standards without thereby creating a stance-independent moral obligation. A change in law may track a change in social values rather than the discovery of an objective moral fact.
- Ordinary "shoulds" can still survive when they are conditional on goals, laws, incentives, or shared values. What the view rejects is a stance-independent categorical moral "ought" that remains authoritative regardless of those conditions.

## Conversational consequence

If the other person accepts these implications, do not keep escalating to increasingly disturbing cases as though one more example will produce a contradiction. This branch has done its job: the implication has been made explicit and accepted.

The next move is a genuinely independent line of argument:

- **Moral fallibility:** can an individual sincerely approve of something and nevertheless be morally mistaken?
- **Categorical reasons:** can someone have a genuine reason not to harm another person even if none of their desires favors restraint?
- **Moral progress:** is a reform better only by our present lights, or can both earlier and later societies be judged by a standard beyond either one's approval?
- **Epistemic normativity:** if there are objective standards for what one ought to believe, why is objective normativity possible there but impossible in morality?

Each branch should then face its strongest anti-realist reply rather than immediately returning to the original society-wide-error example.

## Important limit

An uncomfortable implication is not automatically a logical refutation. The purpose of this endpoint is to identify the cost of the position accurately and then change argumentative dimensions if further discussion is useful.
