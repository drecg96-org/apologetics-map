# Claim

A necessarily good personal foundation may have **comparative explanatory advantages** when several features of moral experience are considered together.

The claim is not that secular moral realism is incoherent, nor that an impersonal moral fact is impossible. It is the narrower proposal that a personal moral ground can potentially explain several kinds of moral data within one account.

## The proposed explanatory package

Theists commonly ask whether one foundation can make sense of:

- **objective value:** why persons, love, justice, and other goods possess worth independently of approval;
- **authoritative obligation:** why some reasons bind an agent even when the agent does not want to comply;
- **personal dignity:** why persons have a kind of worth that is not reducible to usefulness, preference, or social status;
- **moral accountability:** why obligation can have the character of something owed rather than merely an impersonal fact to be noticed.

A theistic account can propose that value is grounded in a necessarily good personal reality and that obligation is related to legitimate personal authority.

## Why this premise is disputed

None of those explanatory claims is automatic.

A secular realist can hold that necessary normative truths exist without God. A naturalist can argue that moral properties are grounded in natural facts about persons or flourishing. A constructivist can try to derive normativity from rational or interpersonal procedures. Critics can also ask why introducing God is more explanatory than treating the ultimate normative facts themselves as basic.

The comparison therefore has to be made against serious alternatives rather than against simple subjectivism.

## Role in the moral argument

This is the **main comparative premise** in the abductive moral argument.

Granting objective morality does not yet establish theism. The further question is whether a personal necessarily good foundation explains the relevant moral data better than its leading competitors.

The argument mapper exposes this premise separately so that objections to it can be evaluated without reopening every question about whether objective moral truths exist at all.
