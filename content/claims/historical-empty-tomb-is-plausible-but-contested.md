# Claim

There is a serious historical case for an empty tomb, but it should not be placed in the same confidence category as Jesus' crucifixion.

## Positive considerations

- early burial tradition;
- the Markan narrative's women discoverers;
- the presence of empty-tomb tradition across the canonical Gospels;
- and the fit between bodily-resurrection proclamation and a missing body.

## Reasons for caution

- Gospel literary dependence limits simple multiple-attestation counts;
- the details develop and vary;
- the historicity and specificity of the burial itself are debated;
- and an empty tomb would still require a separate causal explanation.

The map therefore treats the historical empty tomb as **contested**, not as a fact a skeptic must grant before continuing.
