# Claim

Human beings possess at least some genuine rational thought and knowledge.

The claim is intentionally modest. It does not say that human reasoning is infallible, that every belief is justified, or that skepticism about particular subjects is irrational.

It says there is a real distinction between **reasoning well and reasoning badly**, and that at least some beliefs amount to knowledge rather than merely caused opinion.

## Role in transcendental arguments

A transcendental argument does not normally try to prove rationality from something more basic. It starts with rational activity already being used—even by the critic—and asks what conditions must be true for that activity to be intelligible.
