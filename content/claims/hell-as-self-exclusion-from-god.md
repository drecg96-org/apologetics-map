# Claim

A major Christian response says the picture of God **arbitrarily throwing an otherwise willing person away from himself** is misleading.

If heaven is ultimately communion with God, then a person who finally and persistently refuses God is refusing the central good heaven consists in. On this account, forcing that person into communion would not respect their agency.

C. S. Lewis famously expressed the idea with the image of hell's doors being locked from the inside. Catholic teaching uses the more precise phrase **definitive self-exclusion from communion with God**.

## What this view is trying to explain

It reframes the question from:

> "Why would God torture someone for failing a belief test?"

to:

> "What should a loving God do with a creature who finally refuses communion with him?"

## The key vulnerability

The model works only if the relevant separation is meaningfully **chosen**.

That makes sincere nonbelief a central test case. Someone can fail to believe that God exists without consciously choosing life apart from a God they believe to be real.
