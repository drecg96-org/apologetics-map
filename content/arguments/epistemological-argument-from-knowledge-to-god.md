# Argument

One family of transcendental or epistemological theistic arguments starts with a modest datum:

**Human beings possess at least some genuine knowledge.**

It then asks what must be true for that knowledge to be possible.

Different versions emphasize different alleged preconditions: reliable cognitive faculties, an intelligible world, objective epistemic norms, a sufficiently deep fit between minds and reality, or a comprehensive standpoint that secures the coherence of partial human knowledge.

The conclusion varies accordingly. Some versions argue only toward theism; stronger Van Tilian versions attempt to situate these epistemic conditions within Christian theism.

## Why this node is separate

There is no single agreed "knowledge therefore God" syllogism. James Anderson's survey reconstructs several distinct arguments associated with Plantinga and Van Til. Treating them as a family prevents the map from making one weak premise carry every epistemological burden.

## Main pressure points

A critic can ask whether reliable knowledge must have a personal metaphysical ground; whether evolutionary, reliabilist, naturalist, Platonist, or other accounts supply enough of the required structure; and whether the argument establishes reality or only what we must presuppose in order to reason.
