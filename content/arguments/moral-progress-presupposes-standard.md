# Argument

When we say a society became morally better rather than merely different, we appear to compare both its earlier and later norms against some standard.

If social approval itself constituted moral truth, then a society's approved practices would be correct by definition while they were approved. That makes the language of moral error and moral reform harder to interpret in the ordinary realist way.

## Reply available to anti-realists

An anti-realist can reconstruct moral progress in terms of increasing coherence, reduced suffering, fuller inclusion, or movement toward values we endorse. The argument therefore does not prove stance-independent morality; it asks which account best captures what we mean when we condemn an entire society as morally mistaken.
