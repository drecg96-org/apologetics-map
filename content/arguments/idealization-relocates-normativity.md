# Argument

Idealization solves the problem of actual-attitude fallibility only if the idealizing conditions have genuine normative authority.

If we say a better-informed, more coherent, or more rational standpoint is the one a person **ought** to follow, the central normative question reappears one level up: why are those standards authoritative rather than merely another chosen procedure?

## What this establishes

The point is not that constructivism is incoherent. It is that moving from actual preferences to idealized preferences does not make the need for normative explanation vanish.

If the standards are themselves stance-independent, realism gains ground. If their authority is constructed from agency or deliberation, the constructivist branch remains live.
