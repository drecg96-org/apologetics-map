# Argument

A narrower argument isolates one proposed precondition of rational thought: the laws of logic.

Anderson and Welty defend a form of **theistic conceptual realism**. In broad outline:

1. Logical laws are not merely descriptions of how particular people happen to think; they have a necessary and general character.
2. They are naturally understood as truths about truths or propositions.
3. Propositions are the sorts of things that can function as intentional mental contents.
4. Necessary logical truths therefore call for a necessarily existent mind rather than contingent human minds.
5. A necessarily existent personal spiritual mind is what classical theism calls God.

The argument is useful here because it turns a broad TAG slogan into a narrower claim that can be challenged premise by premise.

## What it does not yet establish

Even if the argument succeeds, its immediate conclusion is closer to **theism** than to the full set of Christian claims about Trinity, incarnation, resurrection, covenant, or Scripture.

It can contribute to a cumulative Christian case without silently doing all of the religion-discriminating work.
