# Argument

If a person can sincerely approve of an action and still be morally wrong about it, moral truth cannot simply be identical to that person's approval.

That does not yet tell us what makes the moral judgment true or false. It only shows that a standard capable of correcting the person's attitude must be distinguished from the attitude itself.

## Subjectivist reply

A subjectivist may appeal to idealized attitudes: what the person would endorse under fuller information, greater coherence, or rational reflection.

That avoids the simplest problem but creates a further question: why do those idealized conditions have normative authority, and are they merely procedural or do they track a standard independent of actual preference?

## Conversational use

This line is especially useful after someone accepts the atrocity implication of subjectivism. Do not repeat more extreme examples. Change dimensions and ask whether genuine moral error is possible.
