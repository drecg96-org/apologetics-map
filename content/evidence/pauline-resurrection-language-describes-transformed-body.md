# Evidence

Paul answers the question "with what kind of body do they come?" by describing continuity and transformation: mortality gives way to immortality, weakness to power, and the present body to a "spiritual body."

Whatever "spiritual body" means, Paul continues to use **body** language rather than replacing resurrection with simple immortality of an immaterial soul.

## Why it matters

This bears on what early Christians meant when they said Jesus was "raised."

If resurrection is embodied, appearance-only theories must also explain why the movement selected resurrection language rather than merely vindication, exaltation, or continued spiritual existence.

## What this does not establish

The passage does not settle how much material continuity Paul envisioned between corpse and resurrection body, nor does it independently establish that Jesus' tomb was empty.

## Main challenges

The interpretation of "spiritual body," continuity, and Paul's relation to the Gospel empty-tomb traditions remains debated.
