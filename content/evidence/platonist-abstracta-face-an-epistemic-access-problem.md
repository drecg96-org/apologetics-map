# Evidence

A Platonist can answer part of the intelligibility problem by treating logical or mathematical truths as objective abstract realities rather than products of human convention.

That move, however, creates a well-known epistemological question: if the relevant abstracta are nonspatiotemporal and causally isolated, **what explains reliable human knowledge of them?**

Benacerraf's work made this tension central in philosophy of mathematics, and contemporary surveys continue to treat epistemic access as a major challenge for Platonism.

## Why it matters

This gives TAG a concrete comparative pressure point rather than a bare assertion that "Platonism cannot account for logic."

A worldview that posits necessary abstract truths still needs an epistemology connecting finite thinkers to those truths.

## What this does not establish

The access problem is not a demonstrated contradiction in Platonism. Platonists have sophisticated responses, and even a failure of one Platonist epistemology would not by itself establish Christian theism.

The evidential force is therefore **comparative and defeasible**, not a proof of universal rival-worldview failure.
