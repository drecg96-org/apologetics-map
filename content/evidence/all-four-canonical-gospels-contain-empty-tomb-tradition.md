# Evidence

Every canonical Gospel contains a narrative in which Jesus' burial place is found without his body.

## Why it matters

The empty-tomb claim is not confined to one late canonical book.

The variation among the narratives also gives historians material for studying development, shared tradition, and possible independent elements.

## What this does not establish

Four canonical narratives do **not** automatically equal four independent witnesses.

Matthew and Luke use Mark extensively, and John's relationship to the Synoptic tradition is separately debated.

## Main challenges

The central historical question is not whether the texts contain empty-tomb stories—they do—but how many independent traditions lie behind them and how reliable those traditions are.
