# Evidence

Some arguments for God from logic speak as though there were one uncontroversially identified package called **the laws of logic**.

Contemporary philosophy of logic is more complicated. Logical pluralists defend the possibility that multiple consequence relations can each be correct under different legitimate specifications.

## Why it matters

This does not make logic arbitrary. It does require the transcendental argument to identify more precisely what needs metaphysical grounding: truth, consequence, necessity, normativity, modal structure, or some combination of these.

A rival view cannot be judged to have failed until the explanandum itself is sufficiently clear.

## What this does not establish

Logical pluralism does not show that logical truth is conventional or subjective, and it does not refute a theistic account of logic.

It raises the precision burden on any argument that infers a unique metaphysical ground from "logic" in the abstract.
