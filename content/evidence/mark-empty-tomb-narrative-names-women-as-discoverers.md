# Evidence

Mark's extant ending places women—including Mary Magdalene—at the discovery of the empty tomb.

The textual proposition is straightforward: this is how the earliest surviving canonical empty-tomb narrative tells the story.

## Why it matters

Defenders argue that naming women as the first discoverers is less likely to be an apologetically convenient invention in that social context and therefore supports historical memory.

## What this does not establish

The presence of women in the story does not by itself prove the tomb was empty. Ancient authors could create narratives involving women, and arguments from embarrassment must be used cautiously.

## Main challenges

The evidential force depends on assumptions about ancient testimony, narrative purpose, and whether the feature was inherited from earlier tradition rather than created by Mark.
