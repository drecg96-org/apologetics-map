# Evidence

A non-Christian Roman historian and early Christian narrative traditions converge on the basic claim that Jesus was executed under Pontius Pilate.

Tacitus, writing in the early second century, says the movement's namesake suffered the extreme penalty during Tiberius' reign under Pilate. The Christian passion traditions independently make Pilate the Roman authority responsible for the execution.

## Why it matters

This convergence makes a theory in which Jesus never underwent Roman execution much harder to sustain.

It supports the relatively modest premise that Jesus actually died by crucifixion before resurrection claims emerged.

## What this does not establish

It does not validate every trial detail in the Gospels, prove the exact charge against Jesus, establish burial, or support resurrection by itself.

## Main challenges

Tacitus writes decades later, and historians still debate which information available to him came from public knowledge, records, or Christians. Those questions affect the evidential independence of details, but not the textual fact that he reports execution under Pilate.
