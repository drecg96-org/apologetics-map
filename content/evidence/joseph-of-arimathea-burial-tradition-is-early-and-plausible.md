# Evidence

The earliest extant Gospel narrative attributes Jesus' burial to Joseph of Arimathea, and substantial historical scholarship regards some form of burial as plausible or likely.

## Why it matters

A burial account gives the empty-tomb question a concrete historical setting and resists theories in which nobody had any idea what happened to the body.

## What this does not establish

It does not prove that every Markan burial detail is historical, that the tomb remained known, or that the tomb was later found empty.

## Main challenges

Roman treatment of crucified bodies varied, Gospel narratives are theological texts as well as historical sources, and scholars dispute how much later burial traditions depend on Mark.
