# Evidence

The early appearance tradition preserved by Paul names **Cephas** and **the Twelve** as recipients of appearances.

## Why it matters

Named recipients make the origin of resurrection belief more historically specific than a vague claim that "some Christians later said Jesus appeared."

The tradition associates appearance claims with central leaders of the earliest movement.

## What this does not establish

A named appearance claim is not equivalent to proof that an external bodily Jesus was objectively present.

## Main challenges

The source does not describe what Cephas or the Twelve experienced, and historians cannot directly inspect their experiences. The evidential dispute concerns whether the best explanation is an external encounter, vision, memory development, group religious experience, or something else.
