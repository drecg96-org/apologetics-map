# Evidence

The 2020 PhilPapers survey asked professional philosophers about metaethics.

Among respondents to the main question:

- **62.07%** accepted or leaned toward moral realism.
- **26.12%** accepted or leaned toward moral anti-realism.
- The remainder selected other or mixed responses.

A related question found **69.25%** accepting or leaning toward cognitivism and **20.72%** toward non-cognitivism.

## What this does show

Objective or realist approaches to morality are not merely a Christian apologetic invention. They are mainstream options in contemporary philosophy, including among many non-theists.

## What this does not show

Philosophers can be wrong, survey populations have selection effects, and majority opinion is not a truth-maker. This node therefore supplies intellectual context rather than a premise from authority.
