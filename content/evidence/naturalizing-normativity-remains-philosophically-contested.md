# Evidence

Reasoning is not exhausted by a causal description of which beliefs people form.

We ordinarily distinguish beliefs and inferences as **justified or unjustified, rational or irrational, warranted or unwarranted**. Those are normative categories.

Philosophical naturalists have proposed several ways to identify, reduce, or otherwise accommodate normativity within a naturalistic ontology. The persistence of normativity objections shows that the bridge from descriptive facts to genuinely normative reasons remains a live grounding problem.

## Why it matters

This is a stronger TAG pressure point than saying merely that "matter cannot think."

The question is whether a worldview can account not only for cognitive events, but also for standards according to which some cognitive transitions count as rationally binding or truth-conducive.

## What this does not establish

The literature does not show that every naturalistic account of normativity has failed. Naturalistic realism, reasons-first views, functional accounts, and other proposals remain live.

So this evidence supports the claim that naturalism bears a **nontrivial explanatory burden**, not the stronger conclusion that naturalism has been decisively refuted.
