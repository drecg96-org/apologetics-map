# Evidence

The inherited material in 1 Corinthians 15 connects four claims in a short sequence:

- Christ died;
- he was buried;
- he was raised;
- he appeared to Cephas and the Twelve.

## Why it matters

The burial and resurrection proclamation appear together in a very early layer of Christian tradition rather than being connected only in later narrative elaboration.

## What this does not establish

The formula does not identify Joseph of Arimathea, describe a tomb, narrate women discovering an empty tomb, or independently prove the resurrection claim.

## Main challenges

The historical truth of each item must be argued separately. There is also debate over whether "was raised" in the formula itself entails an empty tomb.
