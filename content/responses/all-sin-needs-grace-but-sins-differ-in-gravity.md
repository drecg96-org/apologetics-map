# Response

A more defensible Christian formulation is:

> **All sin is serious enough that human beings need God's grace, but not all sins are equally grave or equally culpable.**

That separates several issues that are often collapsed.

## Four different senses of "equal"

Sins can be "equal" in one respect and unequal in another:

- **Need for grace:** every sinner needs reconciliation with God.
- **Moral gravity:** some acts can be worse than others.
- **Culpability:** knowledge, intention, freedom, role, and circumstance can affect responsibility.
- **Consequences and judgment:** Scripture depicts differing consequences and degrees of judgment.

This is why comparing murder with a disputed question in sexual ethics does not require a Christian to say the two acts are morally identical simply because both may be classified as sin within a particular tradition.

## Civil punishment is another category again

Even if two acts are morally wrong, it does not follow that the state should criminalize both, much less punish them equally. Civil law has a narrower institutional role than a complete moral theology.

So the comparison:

**murder is legally punished, while consensual same-sex relationships are not**

does show an important difference in contemporary law. It does **not** generate a contradiction in Christian ethics unless one first assumes either that legal penalties perfectly measure moral gravity or that every sin must receive a corresponding civil penalty.

Christianity need not assume either proposition.

## Apologetic use

When a non-believer says changing punishment proves morality is subjective, separate the questions:

1. Did the **law** change? — yes.
2. Did **social approval** change? — often yes.
3. Does that fact alone show the **moral truth** changed? — no; that is the metaethical issue still under debate.
4. Does Christianity say every sin deserves an identical earthly penalty? — no.

This keeps the argument focused on moral objectivity rather than defending an inaccurate "all sins are equal" slogan.
