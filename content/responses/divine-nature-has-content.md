# Response

A divine-nature theorist need not define 'good' as merely 'whatever God is like.'

The account can point to substantive traits such as justice, faithfulness, benevolence, love, honesty, and opposition to cruelty and betrayal, then argue that these are unified in the character of the necessarily perfect being.

## Remaining pressure

The critic can still ask whether those traits are recognized as good independently of God. The discussion therefore becomes a deeper metaphysical question about whether God explains the unity and authority of value or whether the moral concepts are doing the explanatory work themselves.
