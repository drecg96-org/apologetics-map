# Response

There is an important distinction between:

- a **motivating reason**: what actually moves an agent; and
- a **normative reason**: a consideration that counts in favor of an action.

A cruel or indifferent person may fail to be motivated by another person's suffering. That psychological fact does not by itself settle whether the suffering gives the agent a reason not to cause it.

## Remaining dispute

The internalist can still argue that genuine normative reasons must be connected to what the agent could be motivated by under sound deliberation. The externalist can deny that requirement.

So the debate cannot be settled merely by pointing to motivation; it turns on the theory of normative reasons itself.
