# Response

Before arguing about whether hell is fair, clarify what **going to hell** means in the view being defended.

## Model 1 — judicial punishment

God judges the person and imposes a deserved consequence for sin.

On this model, "God sends people to hell" is not merely misleading shorthand. Divine judgment is doing real explanatory work.

## Model 2 — self-exclusion

The deepest reality of hell is separation from God that the person persistently chooses.

On this model, God does not force someone into loving communion against their will. Hell is the final form of refusing the source of the very communion heaven consists in.

## Model 3 — judgment ratifies choice

These need not be mutually exclusive.

A person can be responsible for a settled rejection of God **and** God can truly judge that rejection. The final judgment confirms and judges an orientation the person has made their own.

## Why the distinction matters

"Punishment" raises questions about desert and proportionality.

"Self-exclusion" raises questions about freedom, knowledge, and whether the person really chose separation.

The good-nonbeliever case puts pressure especially on the second model: **did this person actually reject God, or did they simply fail to believe God exists?**
