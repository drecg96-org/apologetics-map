# Response

A useful correction is to avoid saying that God must literally **give up omnipotence** in order for creatures to be free.

On a libertarian account, the idea is instead that God chooses to create agents whose morally significant choices are not exhaustively determined by God.

That is a restraint on **what God chooses to cause**, not necessarily a reduction in **what God has the power to do**.

## The logical point

Suppose libertarian freedom means that, at the moment of choice, the agent's decision is genuinely up to the agent and not causally determined by God.

Then the demand:

> "God should causally determine the person to choose good while leaving that very choice undetermined by God"

contains conflicting conditions.

Omnipotence is not usually defined as the ability to make contradictions true.

Plantinga's stronger move is that although a world where every free creature always does right may be logically describable, it does not follow that such a world is **feasible for God to actualize** while preserving those same creaturely free choices.

## Important Christian disagreement

This is not the only Christian model of freedom.

Many Christian traditions use compatibilist or strongly providential accounts in which divine sovereignty and responsible human choice are understood differently. So the libertarian defense should be marked as one important apologetic strategy, not as a doctrine every Christian must accept.

## What it still does not answer

Even if this defense succeeds against a logical contradiction, it does not explain every instance of suffering, natural evil, animal pain, disease, disaster, or apparently excessive harm.
