# Response

Some Christians and philosophers of religion reject the claim that **every person's capacity to respond to God ends at death**.

There are several versions:

1. **Postmortem opportunity:** a person may encounter God more clearly after death and still possess meaningful freedom to repent.
2. **Corrective or temporary hell:** postmortem punishment can be remedial rather than everlasting.
3. **Universal reconciliation:** God ultimately succeeds in reconciling every person without simply forcing unwilling people into communion.

These views are not identical and should not be collapsed into one doctrine.

## Biblical arguments used for the view

Supporters often discuss Christ's proclamation to the dead in **1 Peter 3:18–20** and the statement in **1 Peter 4:6** that the gospel was preached to the dead.

Universalist readings also appeal to broad reconciliation texts such as **1 Corinthians 15:22–28** and **Colossians 1:19–20**.

None of those texts straightforwardly settles the question. Their interpretation is heavily disputed, and traditional Catholic and Protestant confessions generally do not infer a new saving opportunity after death from them.

## The next question

If postmortem freedom is possible, the debate does not immediately end.

It becomes:

> Once a person knows much more clearly that God is real, why would they still refuse reconciliation?
