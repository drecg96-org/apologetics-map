# Response

The thought experiment can accidentally assume a model Christianity generally rejects:

**good enough people earn heaven; bad enough people earn hell.**

On classical Christian accounts, salvation is not a prize awarded once a person accumulates enough moral credit. The starting claim is that everyone needs grace.

That matters because the Christian is not forced to say:

> "This person was morally excellent, but God ignored all of that because they failed a belief test."

The Christian can instead say that **nobody is saved because they deserved heaven through moral performance**.

## What this response does not solve

This is an important correction, but it is not yet a complete answer.

The objector can still ask:

> "Fine. Nobody earns heaven. But why would a loving God withhold saving communion from a sincere person who is open to God but honestly unconvinced?"

That pushes the debate toward the nature of hell, human freedom, and culpability for unbelief.
