# Response

The Stroudian objection need not make transcendental reasoning useless.

A more modest argument can ask which commitments are difficult or impossible to abandon while continuing to reason at all. That can expose pressure on a worldview even if it does not immediately prove the full external reality of everything presupposed.

For example, an interlocutor who argues against objective rational norms still has to use norms of valid inference in making the argument. That does not by itself prove Christian theism, but it creates a legitimate explanatory question:

**What account of reality makes sense of the rational norms both sides are already using?**

A modest route can identify rational commitments, compare how rival worldviews account for them, test for self-undermining tensions, and then combine the philosophical comparison with independent historical and revelatory evidence.
