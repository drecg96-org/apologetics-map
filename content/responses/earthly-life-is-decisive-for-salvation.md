# Response

The historically common Catholic and Protestant answer is **no ordinary second chance after death**.

On this view, death is followed by judgment rather than by a new period of evangelism in which a person can revisit the decision under clearer conditions.

The Catechism states this explicitly, and the Westminster Confession gives a parallel Reformed account of the righteous and wicked entering distinct states after death.

## Biblical case

The case is cumulative rather than resting on one proof text:

- **Hebrews 9:27** places judgment after death.
- **Luke 16:19–31** portrays the rich man unable to cross the divide after death.
- **Matthew 25:31–46** presents final judgment as a decisive separation.
- **2 Corinthians 5:10** ties judgment to what was done "in the body."

Together, these texts support the traditional inference that the present life is the ordinary arena of repentance and faith.

## Important qualification

This conclusion is widely held, but it is still an **interpretive theological conclusion**.

It must answer passages such as 1 Peter 3:18–20 and 4:6, and it does not by itself settle every question about infants, the unevangelized, purgatory, or God's treatment of nonculpable ignorance.
