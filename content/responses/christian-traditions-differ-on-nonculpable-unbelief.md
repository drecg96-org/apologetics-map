# Response

This is where the line should stop pretending there is one simple "Christian answer."

## A Catholic inclusivist example

The Catechism teaches that all salvation is through Christ, while also allowing that people who do not know Christ or the Church **through no fault of their own** may attain salvation if they sincerely seek God and respond to grace and conscience.

On that view, explicit propositional belief during earthly life is not treated as the only fact relevant to culpability.

## A stricter Reformed example

The Westminster Confession takes a much more restrictive line. It rejects the idea that diligent moral life or another religion supplies an alternative saving route apart from Christ.

Its larger framework also emphasizes that salvation is entirely the work of divine grace rather than a reward for human moral achievement.

## What follows

The good-nonbeliever objection therefore branches into deeper soteriological questions:

- What counts as culpable rejection?
- Is explicit conscious faith required in every salvific case?
- How should conscience and available revelation matter?
- How does divine grace operate in people who never come to explicit Christian belief?
- What does it mean to say salvation is only through Christ?

Those are genuine tradition-level disagreements and should be mapped separately rather than hidden inside the word "unbeliever."
