# Response

The free-will answer is that **certainty about God's existence is not the same thing as reconciliation with God**.

Christian Scripture itself distinguishes bare recognition from faithful trust. James says even demons believe that God is one, and the Gospels portray hostile spiritual beings recognizing Jesus while opposing him.

That makes the following state conceptually possible:

> "I know God is real, but I still refuse God's authority, forgiveness, transformation, or communion."

## Why this matters to postmortem freedom

If the main obstacle were only lack of evidence, clearer revelation could resolve it.

But if rejection also involves the will, character, loves, and loyalties, then additional information alone need not guarantee repentance.

Revelation 16 uses vivid apocalyptic imagery in which people experience judgment yet still refuse repentance, illustrating the broader biblical theme that disclosure does not mechanically produce love.

## Limit

This response establishes only **possibility**, not permanence.

It does not show that any human being would in fact resist forever under unlimited truthful, loving, noncoercive engagement from God. That is the next universalist challenge.
