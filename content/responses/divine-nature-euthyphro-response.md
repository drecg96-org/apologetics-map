# Response

A contemporary theist need not accept either simple horn of the Euthyphro dilemma.

One influential family of views distinguishes **moral value** from **moral obligation**:

- God's necessarily loving and good nature is proposed as the standard or ground of value.
- Divine commands or requirements, issuing consistently from that nature, are proposed as the ground of obligation.

On this account, goodness is not created by arbitrary choices, because God could not simply make cruelty good by willing it. But goodness is also not an external standard to which God happens to conform; it is grounded in what God necessarily is.

## What this response accomplishes

It blocks the simplest form of the dilemma: "either arbitrary commands or a standard completely external to God."

## What remains open

The critic can still ask whether identifying God's nature as the Good explains goodness or merely relocates the unexplained stopping point. That is a further objection rather than the original two-horn dilemma.
