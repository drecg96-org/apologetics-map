# Response

One answer is that **forgiving a wrong and restoring a relationship are not exactly the same act**.

A person may release resentment or cancel a debt while the relationship itself remains fractured. Reconciliation normally includes some restored relation between persons: trust, communion, repentance, truth-telling, transformation, or renewed participation in a shared good.

Applied to salvation, this means a Christian can say:

- grace originates with God;
- salvation is not earned;
- God can genuinely offer forgiveness;
- and yet restored communion is not merely a legal label applied to a person regardless of that person's orientation.

This is the intuition behind many free-will accounts of hell.

## Important limit

This distinction does **not** prove that some people must be lost forever.

A universalist can agree that reconciliation cannot be reduced to a legal fiction and still argue that God will eventually bring every person to freely accepted reconciliation.

So this response clarifies why "save everyone by default" may oversimplify salvation, but it leaves the final-universalism debate open.
