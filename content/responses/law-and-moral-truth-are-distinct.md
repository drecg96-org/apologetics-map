# Response

Civil law, social acceptance, and moral truth answer different questions.

- **Legal:** What conduct will the state permit, prohibit, or punish?
- **Social:** What conduct does a community approve or disapprove?
- **Moral:** What ought a person to do, and what is actually right or wrong?

Those categories overlap, but they are not identical. A moral realist can therefore say that a society's laws changed while denying that the underlying moral truth changed.

## Why punishment is a poor one-to-one measure of moral gravity

A legal system does not attempt to punish every vice or enforce every moral duty. It also weighs public order, rights, evidence, enforceability, proportionality, jurisdiction, and the proper limits of state coercion.

So two acts receiving different civil penalties does not by itself establish either:

- that one is morally worse in exactly the same proportion, or
- that an unpunished act is morally permissible.

Likewise, repealing a punishment proves that the legal rule changed. Whether the society corrected a moral error or merely changed its standards is a further question.

## Same-sex conduct as an example

Modern legal and cultural acceptance of same-sex relationships can be used as evidence that **social attitudes and laws have changed**. It does not, by itself, decide whether traditional Christian sexual ethics is true or false. That requires a separate substantive argument about sexual morality, Scripture, natural law, human flourishing, and competing Christian interpretations.

Traditional Christian sources also commonly distinguish moral evaluation from civil treatment. For example, the Catholic Catechism condemns same-sex sexual acts within its sexual ethic while also requiring respect for persons and rejecting unjust discrimination. That makes the narrower point especially clear: moral disapproval does not automatically entail a demand for civil punishment.

## Limit

This distinction does not prove any particular Christian moral teaching. It blocks only the inference:

**the law changed → therefore objective moral truth changed.**
