# Response

The strongest Van Tilian answer to the symmetry objection is that the argument is not supposed to proceed:

**reason exists → a generic God exists → choose a religion later.**

It instead compares entire worldviews.

On this approach, Christian claims about the Creator, creation, human beings as image-bearers, sin, revelation, providence, Christ, and redemption form one system. The apologist asks whether that complete system can account for rational experience and whether a rival system can do so on its own terms.

Van Til therefore presents the self-attesting Christ of Scripture, not an unspecified deity, as his starting point. Knudsen likewise describes the intended conclusion as full Christian theism.

## Limit

Saying "compare whole worldviews" is a method, not yet a victory. The Christian still has to demonstrate that some relevant feature of Islam, Judaism, naturalism, or another rival really does fail to account for rationality—and that the alleged failure is not simply defined into the comparison.

That is why this branch should continue into independent historical, textual, and theological discriminators.
