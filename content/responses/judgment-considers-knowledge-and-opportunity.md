# Response

A Christian does not have to assume that **every instance of nonbelief carries exactly the same culpability**.

Several biblical passages make accountability sensitive to knowledge, opportunity, and response. Catholic teaching makes that distinction explicit by allowing that ignorance of Christ can be nonculpable when it is genuinely "through no fault" of the person.

That gives a more careful answer than:

> "They did not believe, therefore they are equally blameworthy."

## What this response can establish

It can support the claim that divine judgment may distinguish:

- deliberate rejection from honest ignorance;
- suppression of evidence from sincere investigation;
- greater knowledge from lesser knowledge; and
- culpable resistance from nonresistant nonbelief.

## What it does not establish

This does **not** prove that every sincere nonbeliever is saved.

It also does not establish a single Christian consensus about how explicit faith, grace, conscience, and final salvation fit together.

Instead, it blocks one overly simple model: **bare absence of belief, considered in isolation, is not obviously the whole story about culpability**.
