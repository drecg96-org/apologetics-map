# Agent summary

Teh and Loke revisit a recent deductive formulation of the moral argument and argue that it can be stated more comprehensively and simply than competing recent formulations. They directly engage a proposed "relaxed moral realism" response and objections concerning the social character of moral obligation, the law-like character of duties, and the relation between moral truths and responsibility.

Their positive conclusion is explicitly theistic: they argue that objective moral truths are metaphysically grounded in a divine personal entity. The paper is therefore especially relevant to the step from accepting moral realism to asking what kind of reality best explains binding obligation.

## How to use this source

This is a recent peer-reviewed defense of the moral argument and is valuable for current debate structure, especially against non-theistic realism. It remains an advocacy source in a contested philosophical literature and should be presented alongside the secular-realist alternatives it criticizes.
