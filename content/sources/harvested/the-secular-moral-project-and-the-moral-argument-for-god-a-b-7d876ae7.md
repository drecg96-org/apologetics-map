# Agent summary

Kratt gives a historical synopsis of moral arguments for God and of the emergence of a more autonomous secular ethical project. The article emphasizes John Stuart Mill, Henry Sidgwick, and G. E. Moore as important figures in the movement toward secular normative ethics and metaethics, while also describing a renewed contemporary interest in theistic metaethics.

For the map, the main value of the article is historical orientation. It helps show that "objective morality without God" is not a single recent objection but part of a long development in ethical theory, and that the modern moral argument has evolved in dialogue with those secular alternatives.

## How to use this source

Use it for history and context rather than as decisive evidence for either theism or secular moral realism. The article is written from a perspective sympathetic to renewed theistic metaethics.
