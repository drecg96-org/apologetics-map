# Agent summary

Wang develops an epistemological evolutionary-debunking challenge to moral realism. The core concern is not simply that evolution influenced human moral psychology, but that the influence appears capable of producing many of our moral beliefs whether or not those beliefs track stance-independent moral truths.

The thesis applies the challenge to both nonnaturalist realism and naturalist realism. Wang allows that ordinary realist moral belief can begin with a defeasible presumption in its favor, but argues that knowledge of evolution can supply an epistemic defeater when the belief-forming process appears insensitive to moral truth. The thesis also argues that the challenge need not generalize to ordinary perceptual beliefs because their evolutionary history differs in relevant ways.

## How to use this source

This is a strong source for the epistemic version of evolutionary debunking. It should be paired with realist replies rather than treated as a settled refutation of objective morality.
