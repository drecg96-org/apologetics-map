# Agent summary

This chapter is a historical study of William R. Sorley's moral argument for God within Bertocci's broader treatment of late British arguments for theism. It establishes Sorley as part of the pre-contemporary history of moral reasoning toward God and is therefore useful for tracing the genealogy of the argument beyond today's divine-command and metaethical formulations.

## Access limitation

The accessible publisher record exposes the chapter's placement and bibliographic context but not enough full text to support a detailed reconstruction of Sorley's argument. Use this source as historical provenance rather than as evidence for a specific premise in the contemporary moral argument.
