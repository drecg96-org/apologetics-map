# Source

Rea treats divine hiddenness as both an intellectual and existential problem.

His central strategy questions whether a perfectly loving transcendent God must relate to creatures exactly as an ideal human parent would. He also argues that divine presence may be more widely available than the hiddenness literature sometimes assumes, including through practices of seeking, lament, worship, and participation.

Especially relevant is his proposal that a person may be able to begin participating in relationship with God even without possessing a fully formed Christian concept of God.

## Important limit

The proposal does not make felt absence unreal. Rea explicitly takes experiences of silence, abandonment, trauma, and failed seeking seriously rather than dismissing them as simple rebellion.
