# Agent summary

O'Connor states that he regards ethical statements such as "murder is wrong" as expressions of attitudes rather than propositions that report stance-independent moral facts. This places his current view in the non-cognitivist / emotivist family rather than simple individual or cultural relativism.

## Why it matters for the map

This is a stronger and more precise alternative to the vague claim that "morality is subjective." An emotivist can strongly condemn cruelty while denying that the condemnation is a truth-apt report about an objective moral property.

## Limitation

The page introduces O'Connor's own position and a conversation with Simon Blackburn. It is a perspective source, not neutral evidence that emotivism is correct.
