# Agent summary

Morriston grants that a divine-nature account can avoid crude arbitrariness, then asks why properties such as love and justice are good. The objection pressures the claim that identifying God with the Good supplies deeper explanation rather than relocating the stopping point.

## How to use this source

Use after the standard divine-nature Euthyphro reply, not as a restatement of the original dilemma.
