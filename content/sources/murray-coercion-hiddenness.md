# Source

Murray argues that divine hiddenness may serve morally significant freedom.

The strongest version of the response is not that merely **believing God exists** would mechanically eliminate freedom. Rather, vivid awareness of an omniscient moral judge together with overwhelming awareness of reward and punishment could place some people under powerful practical pressure to behave in prescribed ways.

That gives a possible reason for some epistemic distance.

## Important limit

This defense is contested. Clear evidence of someone's existence does not ordinarily force love, trust, obedience, or allegiance, so the argument must explain why divine disclosure would be coercive in a stronger way than ordinary knowledge.
