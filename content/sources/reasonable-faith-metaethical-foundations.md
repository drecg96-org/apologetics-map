# Agent summary

Craig explicitly separates the question of whether non-theists can live morally from the question of what, if anything, grounds objective moral facts. His positive case argues that a theistic worldview can ground moral value in God's nature, moral duties in divine requirements, and ultimate moral accountability in divine judgment.

For the map, the most useful contribution is the **grounding distinction**: moral behavior and moral knowledge do not by themselves settle moral ontology.

## How to use this source

This is an advocacy source defending theistic metaethics, not neutral evidence that theism has won the philosophical dispute. It should be paired with secular moral realism, naturalistic accounts, error theory, and other rival explanations already represented in the graph.
