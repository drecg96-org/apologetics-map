# Source

Schellenberg develops the strongest contemporary form of the divine-hiddenness argument.

The key category is **nonresistant nonbelief**: a person who is capable of relationship with God, is not culpably resisting that relationship, and nevertheless does not believe that God exists.

The argument presses a claim about divine love rather than merely about the amount of public evidence. If a perfectly loving God is always open to meaningful relationship with every capable and nonresistant person, Schellenberg argues, then such a person should not be left unable to begin that relationship through nonbelief.

## Best used for

Use this source when stating the hiddenness objection in its strongest form rather than reducing it to "why doesn't God perform more miracles?" or "why isn't God obvious to everyone?"
