# Source

This source is useful when a debate treats unbelief as though a person can simply decide, at will, to believe a proposition.

The article distinguishes **direct** voluntary control over belief from **indirect** control through choices such as what evidence to investigate, what sources to consult, and how one responds to reasons. Direct doxastic control remains controversial, while indirect influence is much less controversial.

That distinction matters for culpability. Even if a person cannot simply choose belief on command, they may still bear some responsibility for inquiry, avoidance, intellectual habits, or resistance. The philosophical question is therefore not exhausted by asking whether belief itself is a voluntary act.
