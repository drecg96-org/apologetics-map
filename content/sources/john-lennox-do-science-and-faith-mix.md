# Agent summary

Lennox presents science and Christian faith as compatible rather than competing explanatory systems. He emphasizes the history, scope, and limits of science and argues that moving from successful scientific explanations to atheism is a philosophical inference rather than a result supplied by science itself.

## Useful debate moves

- Distinguish explanations of mechanisms from metaphysical explanations of why an intelligible universe exists.
- Ask whether methodological naturalism in science entails philosophical naturalism.
- Avoid a God-of-the-gaps formulation; the argument concerns the existence and intelligibility of the whole system, not merely unexplained anomalies.

## Limitation

This is an apologetic presentation, not a neutral survey of philosophy of science. Compare it with naturalist and atheist philosophers of science before treating its conclusions as established.
