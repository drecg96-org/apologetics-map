# Agent summary

Cuneo presents a positive argument for moral realism by linking moral and epistemic normativity. The book's core structure is that if moral facts do not exist, then epistemic facts do not exist; epistemic facts do exist; therefore moral facts exist, and moral realism follows.

The crucial work is the proposed parallel between moral and epistemic facts. Cuneo argues that features often invoked against moral realism also appear in epistemic normativity, so a general objection to irreducible normativity risks spreading into skepticism about reasons for belief.

The book goes beyond a modest objection to moral anti-realism: it develops the companions-in-guilt strategy into a positive realist argument and engages epistemic nihilism, expressivism, and reductionism as possible ways to resist it.

## Limitations and use

Use this source to ground the epistemic-normativity / companions-in-guilt branch and to distinguish a modest defeater-defeater from Cuneo's stronger positive argument.

The conclusion depends on defending both the parity between moral and epistemic normativity and the existence of objective epistemic facts. It does not establish a theistic ground for morality and does not show that every form of moral anti-realism fails.
