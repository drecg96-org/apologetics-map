# Source

Blanton argues that **nonresistant nonbelief is relatively common**, strengthening a premise often contested in the divine-hiddenness debate.

The paper distinguishes:

- **acquisition responsibility** — whether a person's own relevant choices or omissions caused them to come to lack belief in God; and
- **maintenance responsibility** — whether later choices or omissions help sustain that nonbelief.

Blanton argues that Schellenberg-style resistance requires the first kind in the relevant sense, and that many nonbelievers lack it. On that account, their nonbelief cannot simply be redescribed as volitional opposition to God.

The article was published online in October 2025 and appears in *Religious Studies* 62(3), September 2026, pp. 547–567.

## Best used for

Use this source when a response to divine hiddenness assumes that genuine nonresistant nonbelief is rare or nonexistent. It does not establish the entire hiddenness argument; it focuses on the existence and prevalence of the phenomenon the argument seeks to explain.
