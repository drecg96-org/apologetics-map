# Agent summary

This Stanford Encyclopedia entry surveys moral arguments for God's existence as a family rather than a single argument. It distinguishes theoretical arguments, which begin from alleged moral facts and argue that God is necessary or explanatorily superior, from practical arguments that reason from goods or ends morality requires.

The entry also supplies historical context from Aquinas and Kant through modern divine-command and cumulative-case approaches. Its treatment makes clear that evaluating a moral argument requires engaging substantive metaethical questions: whether objective moral facts exist, what explains them, how moral obligation is related to divine-command theories, and whether secular alternatives can explain the relevant phenomena.

## How to use this source

This is a strong orientation source for the whole moral-argument branch because it is explicitly survey literature and presents the debate as contested. It should organize the landscape rather than be treated as an authority settling the conclusion.
