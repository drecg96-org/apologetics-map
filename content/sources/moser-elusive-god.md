# Source

Moser reframes what evidence from a perfectly loving and morally authoritative God should be expected to accomplish.

Instead of assuming that God would maximize detached or "spectator" evidence of bare existence, Moser argues that divine self-revelation may be ordered toward repentance, moral transformation, and relationship. Evidence can therefore be **purposively available** rather than maximally coercive or merely intellectually satisfying.

## Important limit

This approach must not simply redefine every sincere nonbeliever as secretly resistant. The hiddenness problem remains strongest precisely where a person appears willing to know and respond to God but does not find the evidence sufficient for belief.
