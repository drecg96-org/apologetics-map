# Source profile

The entry surveys major versions of moral naturalism and objections to them, including the concern that reducing normativity to descriptive natural facts may fail to preserve genuinely normative force.

Although its focus is moral rather than specifically epistemic normativity, the debate is relevant to broad TAG claims because rational thought itself involves standards about what one ought to believe or infer.

## How to use this source

Use it to establish a real naturalistic grounding problem without pretending the problem is already settled. Naturalists have developed reductionist, reasons-first, neo-Aristotelian, and other proposals precisely to answer this sort of challenge.

## Limitation

A live objection to naturalistic reduction is not evidence that all forms of naturalism fail, and it does not by itself support a uniquely Christian explanation.
