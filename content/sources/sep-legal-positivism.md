# Source

Useful for separating two questions that are often conflated in moral debates: **what the law currently is** and **whether that law is morally justified**.

The entry explains that legal positivism treats the existence and content of law as dependent on social facts, while allowing laws to be morally good, bad, just, or unjust. The apologetic use here is limited: the source does not prove moral realism, but it shows that distinguishing legality from morality is standard jurisprudential territory rather than an ad hoc religious move.
