# Objection

Grant that a person **can** resist God even with much clearer knowledge.

Why assume that resistance must remain **forever**?

A universalist can distinguish two claims:

1. God never coerces a person's will.
2. God never stops truthfully, lovingly, and patiently working for that person's reconciliation.

If opportunities continue without limit, the universalist argues that eventual free repentance may be possible—or even expected—without God overriding freedom.

The Stanford Encyclopedia surveys an "infinite opportunity" version of this argument: an unending sequence of genuine opportunities may make permanent refusal increasingly difficult to explain.

## Biblical pressure

Universalists connect this philosophical argument with texts describing God's desire to save, divine patience, universal confession of Christ, and Christ's final victory.

Traditionalists answer that these passages do not guarantee universal salvation and that Scripture also contains severe warnings of final judgment.

## Where the debate lands

The deepest disagreement is no longer simply **"Can people choose?"**

It is:

> Can created freedom become permanently fixed against God, or can perfect divine love eventually heal every freely maintained resistance without coercion?

That question remains genuinely disputed among Christian theologians and philosophers of religion.
