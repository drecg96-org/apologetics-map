# Objection

Suppose the theist says that God does not arbitrarily choose what counts as good because God's necessarily loving, just, and faithful nature is itself the standard.

The critic can then ask what explanatory work the word **good** is doing.

- If God's nature is good simply because it is God's nature, the account may look definitional.
- If love, justice, or mercy are recognized as good for independent reasons, those reasons may appear to do the normative work.
- If every worldview must eventually stop with some necessary feature of reality, a secular realist may ask why necessary moral facts are a worse stopping point than a necessarily good divine nature.

## The live comparison

The issue is no longer the simple Euthyphro dilemma. It is a comparison of theoretical virtues: explanatory depth, simplicity, fit with obligation and agency, and whether either worldview has an illegitimate brute fact.
