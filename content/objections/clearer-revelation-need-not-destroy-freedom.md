# Objection

The freedom defense can blur two different things:

1. **epistemic freedom** — whether I believe that God exists;
2. **volitional freedom** — what I do in response to God.

Clear evidence may settle the first without determining the second.

Christian Scripture itself contains examples in which recognition does not produce allegiance. James says even demons believe that God is one. The Gospels portray hostile spirits recognizing Jesus. Revelation portrays people experiencing what the text presents as divine judgment while still refusing repentance.

So this is conceptually coherent:

> "I know God exists, but I do not love, trust, worship, or obey him."

## Pressure is not the same as impossibility

Murray's response can still say that vivid knowledge of divine reward and punishment would place **powerful pressure** on behavior.

But that is a narrower claim than saying clear revelation eliminates freedom altogether.

The hiddenness defense therefore owes an explanation of degree:

- Why not make God's existence clear while leaving God's full intentions or consequences less overwhelming?
- Why not give a sincere seeker enough evidence for belief without making every moral decision feel coerced?
- If God can reveal himself clearly to prophets, apostles, angels, demons, or people in biblical narratives without eliminating all resistance, why would ordinary human freedom require nonbelief?

This objection does not prove that God has no reason for hiddenness.

It shows that **free will by itself is not yet a complete explanation of nonresistant nonbelief**.
