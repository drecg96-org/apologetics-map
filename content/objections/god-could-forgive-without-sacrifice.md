# Objection

If a human creditor can forgive a debt without demanding that somebody else pay it, an omnipotent and perfectly loving God should seem at least as capable of doing so.

That puts pressure on any atonement account that sounds like:

1. God wants to forgive.
2. A rule requires punishment or payment first.
3. God cannot waive the rule.
4. Jesus must therefore suffer so God is finally able to forgive.

The obvious question is: **who or what makes that rule binding on God?**

If the answer is "God himself," the skeptic can ask why God could not choose mercy without first arranging suffering.

## Why this is a serious objection

This is not merely an objection to Christianity in general. It especially targets theories in which punishment or satisfaction appears to be a prerequisite that constrains divine forgiveness.

Other Christian models understand the cross in terms of victory, healing, representation, reconciliation, revelation of divine love, or some combination of motifs. Those models face their own questions, but they show why the graph should not assume that every Christian means the same thing by "Jesus paid the price."
