# Objection

Societies plainly change their laws, punishments, and standards of social acceptance. Conduct once criminalized may later become legal or protected, while conduct once tolerated may later be prohibited.

A natural inference is:

1. societies decide what conduct to punish or permit;
2. those judgments change over time;
3. therefore morality itself is created by society and changes with social acceptance.

The example can feel especially forceful when comparing conduct that remains severely punished, such as murder, with conduct whose legal treatment has changed dramatically.

## What the example actually establishes

Changing law is strong evidence that **social and legal norms change**. It does not by itself establish the further metaethical claim that **moral truth is constituted by those norms**.

That additional step is the issue in dispute.

## Crux

Ask: if a society legalizes, criminalizes, approves, or condemns something, does that make the act morally right or wrong — or can the law itself be morally mistaken?

If law can be unjust, then legal status and moral truth are not identical.
