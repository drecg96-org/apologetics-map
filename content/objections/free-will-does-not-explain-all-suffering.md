# Objection

Grant the free-will defense for human wrongdoing.

That still leaves:

- earthquakes, storms, disease, and congenital suffering;
- animal pain that is not plausibly caused by human choices;
- harms vastly larger than what seems necessary for meaningful agency;
- and cases of suffering for which no outweighing good is visible.

So the free-will defense can at most be **one component** of a response to evil.

## Why the distinction matters

Plantinga-style arguments are especially important against the claim that **God and any evil whatsoever are logically incompatible**.

The evidential problem asks something harder:

> Given the kinds, amount, and distribution of suffering we actually observe, is a perfectly good and powerful God still the best explanation?
