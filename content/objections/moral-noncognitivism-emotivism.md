# Objection

A critic need not say that each person's moral opinion is "true for them." A more radical option is that ordinary moral utterances are not reports of objective facts at all.

On an emotivist picture, saying "cruelty is wrong" can function as condemnation, disapproval, or a practical commitment rather than as a proposition that detects a moral property in the world.

## Why this is stronger than simple relativism

The emotivist can condemn torture, campaign against injustice, and reason about consistency while denying that moral sentences work like descriptive claims such as "water freezes at 0°C at standard pressure."

So asking "what makes your morality objectively true?" may simply assume the realist semantics that the non-cognitivist rejects.

## Pressure point for the non-cognitivist

The cost is explaining the apparently truth-directed features of moral discourse: disagreement, moral reasoning, apparent error, progress, and the intuition that some judgments would remain mistaken even under unanimous approval.
