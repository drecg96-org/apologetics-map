# Objection

Atheists can know moral truths, care about other people, make sacrifices, and live morally serious lives. Religious belief is clearly not a prerequisite for ordinary moral knowledge or moral behavior.

That observation is important because crude presentations of the moral argument sometimes imply otherwise.

## Where the disagreement actually lies

The sophisticated moral argument does not deny this empirical point. Its question is ontological: what makes moral truths true and obligations binding?

So this objection is decisive against 'you need belief in God to be good,' but not against every grounding argument involving God.
