# Objection

Suppose we establish a complete set of descriptive facts about conscious creatures: which conditions cause pain, which support flourishing, which preferences are stable, and which institutions promote cooperation.

A further question remains: **why ought an agent promote those states?**

The concern is not that flourishing is irrelevant. It is that an account can smuggle normativity into its starting assumptions by defining the moral good in terms of a natural property without yet explaining why that property has authoritative normative force.

## Possible naturalist reply

A moral naturalist can deny that an additional bridge is needed. If moral properties are identical with or constituted by certain natural properties, then asking for a separate "ought" may misunderstand the theory.

The dispute is therefore partly about reduction and explanation, not just about whether suffering and flourishing are real natural facts.
