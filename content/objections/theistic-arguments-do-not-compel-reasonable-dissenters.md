# Objection

A skeptic does not need to claim that arguments for God are foolish.

A more serious position is:

> "I can see why a reasonable theist finds these arguments suggestive, but I do not think their premises or explanatory comparisons rationally require me to accept the conclusion."

That can happen at several levels:

- a premise is disputed,
- an inference is valid but not decisive,
- a proposed explanation is not clearly better than alternatives,
- background probabilities differ,
- or an argument reaches generic theism without reaching Christianity.

## Why this matters conversationally

"Here is an argument for God" and "therefore your nonbelief is irrational" are different claims.

The next branch should identify the actual argument—cosmological, fine-tuning, moral, ontological, consciousness, reason, religious experience, and so on—and examine the strongest skeptical reply to that argument on its own terms.
