# Objection

Error theory agrees with moral realists about one important feature of ordinary moral language: moral claims present themselves as objective claims that can be true or false.

The error theorist then denies that the world contains the objective normative properties those claims require. On this view, ordinary positive moral judgments are systematically in error.

## Why someone might accept it

Classic motivations include persistent disagreement and the apparent metaphysical strangeness of objective prescriptive properties.

The cost is revisionary: many ordinary moral claims we take to be straightforwardly true would turn out to be false.
