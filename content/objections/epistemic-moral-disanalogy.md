# Objection

Granting objective norms for belief does not by itself show that objective moral norms exist.

The critic can argue that epistemic and moral norms differ in relevant respects: their connection to truth, voluntariness, blame, desert, action guidance, or the roles they play in reasoning.

If those differences matter, the inference from "there are epistemic oughts" to "there are moral oughts" may fail.

## Burden created

The companions-in-guilt defender must do more than point out that both domains use the word **ought**. The argument needs a principled reason the anti-realist's objection to moral normativity would also undermine epistemic normativity.
