# Objection

Imagine a person who:

- is unusually honest, compassionate, generous, and willing to admit wrongdoing;
- sincerely seeks truth rather than avoiding religious evidence;
- does not believe God exists because they remain unconvinced;
- would want relationship with God if they became convinced God exists; and
- dies still unconvinced.

If that person is excluded from heaven **simply because they did not believe**, the result can look arbitrary or disproportionate. Their moral character and openness to truth seem disconnected from the decisive criterion.

## Why "perfectly good" is not the strongest wording

A Christian can answer the literal phrase "perfectly good person" by saying Christianity denies that any ordinary human being is sinless.

That is true inside the Christian framework, but it does not make the underlying objection disappear.

The stronger version is therefore the **morally admirable, nonresistant nonbeliever**. The issue is not whether the person has ever sinned. The issue is whether sincere lack of belief should be treated as a culpable rejection deserving final separation from God.

## Debate discipline

Do not quietly change this person into someone who knows God exists and hates him. That would answer a different case.
