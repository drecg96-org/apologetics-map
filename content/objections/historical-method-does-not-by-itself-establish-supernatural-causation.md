# Objection

A skeptic can grant substantial historical claims without granting a resurrection miracle.

For example, they might accept that Jesus was crucified, that early followers sincerely believed he had been raised, or that some followers had experiences interpreted as appearances. The methodological question is what kind of conclusion a historian is entitled to draw from those data.

On one skeptical approach, historical method can compare ordinary historical explanations but cannot conclude:

> "God raised Jesus from the dead."

because supernatural agency is not the kind of cause the method is equipped to establish.

## The real dispute

This is not merely a dispute over one fact. It is a dispute over **method**.

A complete resurrection branch should therefore distinguish:

1. which historical facts are actually well supported,
2. which natural explanations remain live,
3. whether historians must be methodologically naturalistic,
4. and whether an inference-to-the-best-explanation framework can rationally include supernatural hypotheses.

That prevents the debate from becoming "historians say X" when the deeper disagreement is about what historical reasoning is allowed to conclude.
