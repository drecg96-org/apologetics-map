# Objection

A person's actual approval need not be the final standard.

A constructivist can distinguish what someone presently wants from what the person or a suitably characterized rational agent would endorse under better information, greater coherence, reflection, or fair deliberative conditions.

That lets the theory explain ordinary moral error: a present judgment can be wrong because it fails the relevant idealized procedure.

## Why this matters

The fallibility argument successfully pressures **simple actual-attitude subjectivism**, but it does not by itself eliminate more sophisticated standpoint-dependent theories.

The next question is where the authority of the idealizing conditions comes from.
