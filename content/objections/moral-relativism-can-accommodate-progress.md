# Objection

The claim that relativism cannot speak coherently about moral progress is too strong.

A relativist can say that an earlier social practice was wrong **from the standpoint by which we now assess it** and that moving away from it was progress relative to that standpoint. Assessment-sensitive views make room for sincere correction without adding a stance-independent moral standard.

David Wong's pluralistic relativism adds another complication: not every socially accepted morality automatically counts as adequate. Shared facts about human beings, agency, cooperation, and flourishing can constrain the range of viable moral systems even if more than one morality remains defensible.

## What this blocks

This reply blocks a quick inference from the existence of progress language directly to moral realism. The key step still needs argument.

## Remaining pressure point

The debate becomes sharper if we ask whether a reform is merely better **by our present lights**, or whether the earlier society was mistaken even by a standard that does not depend on either society's approval.
