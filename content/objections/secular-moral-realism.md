# Alternative

The moral argument does not get from objective morality to God merely by ruling out relativism. A non-theist can accept robust objective moral facts.

Erik Wielenberg, for example, develops a non-theistic normative realism in which some moral facts are necessary and do not depend on God.

## Place in the grounding debate

This is not an upstream objection to moral realism. It is a **competing realist answer** to the grounding question.

So the debate can branch directly from "what grounds objective morality?" into:

- theistic grounding;
- robust secular realism;
- naturalistic realism;
- other realist or constructivist accounts.

If the theistic argument has already been presented, secular realism also functions as a direct objection to its claim of explanatory superiority.

## Why this matters

It should be kept separate from atheistic **anti-realism**. Emotivism and robust secular realism are both non-theistic positions, but they disagree at the first step about whether moral propositions are objectively true at all.

The comparative questions then include:

- Are necessary normative facts metaphysically acceptable?
- Does obligation require a personal authority?
- Can impersonal normative reasons be genuinely binding?
- Does either view explain moral knowledge better?
- Is God a genuine explanation or another unexplained necessary reality?
- Which theory has the lower explanatory cost?

A serious moral argument must engage these questions rather than treating secular realism as unavailable.
