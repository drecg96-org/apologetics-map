# Alternative

A secular moral theory need not reduce morality to preference. Some realists argue that moral truths are identical with or grounded in natural facts about flourishing, suffering, rational agency, human nature, or reasons for action.

If such an account can explain both value and genuine obligation without appealing to God, the comparative premise of the moral argument loses force.

## Place in the grounding debate

Like robust secular realism, moral naturalism belongs **after** the realism question has been provisionally granted.

It is therefore a direct answer to "what grounds objective morality?" as well as an objection to the claim that only theism can do the explanatory work.

## The crux

The debate is not whether flourishing matters. The harder question is whether descriptive facts about flourishing are sufficient to constitute genuinely normative conclusions about what anyone *ought* to do, including an agent who rejects the relevant goals.

That creates an explicit **is–ought branch**. Critics argue that a theory cannot simply move from facts about what improves conscious lives to an authoritative obligation to promote those outcomes.

Naturalists can reply that if moral properties are identical with or constituted by relevant natural properties, demanding a separate bridge may beg the question against moral naturalism.

## What to compare

A fair comparison should ask whether the theory explains:

- objective value;
- categorical reasons or obligations;
- moral knowledge;
- apparent supervenience between moral and natural facts;
- why the selected natural property is normatively privileged.
