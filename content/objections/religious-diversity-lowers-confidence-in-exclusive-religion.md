# Objection

The argument is not:

> "Many religions exist, therefore all religions are false."

That would be too quick.

The stronger challenge is epistemic. Across cultures and traditions, people who appear sincere, intelligent, informed, and spiritually serious arrive at incompatible conclusions about revelation, salvation, scripture, miracles, and the nature of God.

If those peers disagree, why should one person remain highly confident that their inherited or chosen tradition is uniquely correct?

## The pressure point

Religious diversity can be especially challenging when:

- religious affiliation strongly tracks geography and upbringing,
- rival traditions report apparently analogous religious experiences,
- the traditions make mutually incompatible truth claims,
- and no shared public method seems to settle the disagreement.

A Christian response can appeal to independent historical evidence, cumulative explanatory power, internal coherence, revelation, or reasons why peer disagreement does not require equal confidence. But it should first answer the serious version: **why is confidence in Christianity epistemically justified despite the existence of informed and sincere rivals?**
