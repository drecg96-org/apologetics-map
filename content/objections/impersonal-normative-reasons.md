# Objection

The statement "you have a reason not to inflict pointless suffering" may be objective even if no person issued the reason as a command.

A secular realist can therefore reject the inference from **binding** to **personally commanded**. On this view, some normative reasons or moral facts are necessary features of reality, analogous in one respect to logical or mathematical truths.

## Why this matters

Theistic arguments often gain force by treating obligation as inherently social: if there is a duty, there must be someone to whom the duty is owed or some authority who imposes it.

This objection targets that premise directly. The question becomes whether obligation is fundamentally relational or whether objective normative reasons can bind impersonally.

## Cost of the view

The secular realist still owes an account of why these facts exist, what makes them normative rather than merely descriptive, and how finite agents can know them.
