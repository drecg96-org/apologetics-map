# Objection

A critic need not choose between **physical reductionism** and **theism**.

A secular realist can instead hold that some normative truths or reasons are fundamental features of reality. On such a view, facts about what one ought to believe, what counts as evidence, or which inferences are rational need not be reducible to causal descriptions—but neither must they be commands or thoughts in a divine mind.

## Why this matters

The mere existence of irreducible epistemic normativity therefore does not establish the rival-worldview-failure premise.

To rule out this alternative, TAG needs a further argument that impersonal or fundamental normative facts are impossible, internally incoherent, or explanatorily unacceptable in a way the proposed theistic stopping point is not.

## Cost of the objection

Reasons fundamentalism deliberately stops explanation at the normative level. A TAG defender can press whether this leaves the unity of truth, normativity, rational minds, and an intelligible world unexplained.

But an explanatory stopping point is not automatically a contradiction. The comparison has to show why one stopping point is rationally defective and the other is not.
